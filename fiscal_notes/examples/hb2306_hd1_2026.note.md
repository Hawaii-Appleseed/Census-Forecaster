## What is scored

- **Section 1**: Repeals the scheduled 2027 and 2029 bracket adjustments (bracketed schedules). The 2025 schedule stays in force, with the top three rates raised by one point each (8.25%->10%, 9%->11%, 10%->12% in the single schedule, and the equivalent steps in the other schedules) for tax years beginning after 12/31/2026. The 2029 schedule is repealed too, so there is a single vintage for 2027-2031. (spec: `income_tax.bracket_vintages`; applies from tax year 2027)
  > "Over $450,000 but $32,932.00 plus 10.00% of not over $550,000 excess over $450,000"
- **Section 3**: Moves the sunset of Act 163, SLH 2023 from 12/31/2027 to 12/31/2032. This keeps the EITC at 40% of federal and the larger food/excise credit table through 2031. The dependent care credit part of Act 163 cannot be modeled. (spec: `credits.eitc_percent`, `credits.food_excise`; applies from tax year 2027)
  > "on December 31, [2027,] 2032, this Act shall be repealed"

## What is NOT scored

- **Section 2**: Raises the household and dependent care services credit's applicable percentage: 50% for AGI up to $80,000, falling in steps to 5% over $160,000. Adds a disallowance period after a fraud finding (10 taxable years) or after a final decision disallowing the claim (2 taxable years). The credit cannot be modeled. (not modeled)
- **Section 2**: Disallowance periods: 10 taxable years after a final decision that a claim was due to fraud, and 2 taxable years after a final decision disallowing a claim. This is an enforcement provision for the dependent care credit, which cannot be modeled. (not modeled)

The estimate leaves these provisions out. It does not count their effect on revenue, in either direction.

## Reviewer notes

Section 5 sets the effective date to July 1, 3000, which looks like a placeholder (HD1 draft). The model treats the bill as applying from tax year 2027, as sections 1 and 2 and the description state. Section 2 applies to taxable years beginning after 12/31/2026 and is repealed on 12/31/2032, so it sunsets after the modeled window. The description says the bill 'repeals future adjustments to income tax brackets': the bracketed 2027 and 2029 schedules are removed. As a result the 2024 schedule remains in force for 2027-2031, with the top three rates raised. The new top brackets keep the 2025 thresholds. For single filers the rates are 10% over $225,000, 11% over $275,000 and 12% over $325,000. The bill does not change the standard deduction, personal exemption or capital gains rate, so those are omitted. Section 1 leaves the schedules for 2017 and 2024 in place. Only the schedule headed 'after December 31, 2026' applies from 2027.

## Method and provenance

- Baseline: current law, Act 24 (SLH 2026). Tax years 2027-2031. Change in revenue, $ millions; positive means more revenue.
- Income tax is scored on the simulator population (LOW, MID and HIGH scenarios), static and after behavioral response (`TAX_SIMULATOR_SCOPE.md`).
- Credits are priced from the working-family credits run, not from the simulator population, and are the same in every scenario (run commit `2cba8d0`).
- Rate cuts get no behavioral response: the model's response to a change in the top rate is asymmetric by design, so it applies to rises and not to cuts (`TAX_SIMULATOR_SCOPE.md`, decision 3). When a bill cuts rates, the MID figure can fall outside the LOW-HIGH range.
- The top tail is thin: 49 sample records (MID, tax year 2027) stand for the roughly 2,300 returns above $1 million (`TAX_SIMULATOR_SCOPE.md`). Read the LOW-HIGH range, not the single MID figure, for changes that touch only the top.
